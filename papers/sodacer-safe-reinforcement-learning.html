Self-Organizing Dual-Buffer Adaptive Clustering Experience Replay (SODACER) for safe reinforcement learning in optimal control

Roya Khalili-Amirabadi · Mohsen Jalaeian-Farimani · Omid Solaymani-Fard

Scientific Reports · Vol. 16 · Article 14960 · 2026

Read at publisher ↗Citation and BibTeX ↓
Article abstract

Abstract

This paper proposes a novel reinforcement learning framework, named Self-Organizing Dual-buffer Adaptive Clustering Experience Replay (SODACER), designed to achieve safe and scalable optimal control of nonlinear systems. The proposed SODACER mechanism consists of a Fast-Buffer for rapid adaptation to recent experiences and a Slow-Buffer equipped with a self-organizing adaptive clustering mechanism to maintain diverse and non-redundant historical experiences. The adaptive clustering mechanism dynamically prunes redundant samples, optimizing memory efficiency while retaining critical environmental patterns. The approach integrates SODACER with Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) to guarantee safety by enforcing state and input constraints throughout the learning process. To enhance convergence and stability, the framework is combined with the Sophia optimizer, enabling adaptive second-order gradient updates. The proposed SODACER-Sophia’s architecture ensures reliable, effective, and robust learning in dynamic, safety-critical environments, offering a generalizable solution for applications in robotics, healthcare, and large-scale system optimization. The proposed approach is validated on a nonlinear Human Papillomavirus (HPV) transmission model with multiple control inputs and safety constraints. Comparative evaluations against random and clustering-based experience replay methods demonstrate that SODACER achieves faster convergence, improved sample efficiency, and a superior bias-variance trade-off, while maintaining safe system trajectories, validated via the Friedman test.

Research framing

Research problem

Constrained nonlinear optimal control with reinforcement learning requires recent experience for rapid adaptation, older diverse experience for stability, and explicit safety enforcement.

Technical substance

Main contributions

  • A functionally asymmetric Fast-Buffer / Slow-Buffer replay design, combining recent experiences with diverse historical samples.
  • Self-organizing adaptive clustering and redundancy removal in the Slow-Buffer for memory management.
  • Integration with Control Barrier Functions as a safety filter and a Sophia-style optimizer for policy updating.

Methodological overview

Recent fast buffer→Clustered slow buffer→Replay sampling→Sophia policy update→CBF safety filter

What the article establishes

Evidence and reported findings

  • Numerical evaluation uses a constrained nonlinear Human Papillomavirus (HPV) transmission model, with comparisons to random and clustering-based replay methods.
  • The paper reports convergence, sample efficiency, bias–variance performance, and a Friedman statistical comparison.
  • Safety metrics are reported across five model-control scenarios; the study reports zero constraint violations for SODACER–Sophia within its evaluated scenarios.
Responsible interpretation

Scope and limitations

The empirical case study is a nonlinear HPV transmission model. Broader statements about robotics or healthcare are potential application areas rather than additional physical-robot experiments. Formal conclusions depend on the stated model, safety-filter feasibility, and analytical assumptions.

Research discovery

Research topics where this publication is relevant

  • A safe actor–critic learning system uses Control Barrier Functions together with experience replay.
  • Replay buffers must balance short-term adaptation, non-redundant historical diversity, and learning efficiency.
