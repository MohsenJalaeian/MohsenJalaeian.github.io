Experience replay in reinforcement learning: A systematic review

Roya Khalili Amirabadi · Mohsen Jalaeian Farimani · Omid Solaymani Fard

Neurocomputing · Vol. 700 · Article 134403 · 2026

Read at publisher ↗Citation and BibTeX ↓
Article abstract

Abstract

Background: Experience Replay (ER) has become a cornerstone of modern reinforcement learning (RL), enabling improved sample efficiency and training stability by reusing past experiences. Since its introduction, a diverse set of ER methods has emerged, including prioritized, hindsight, reservoir-based, episodic, and compressed replay schemes. While several narrative surveys exist, a systematic, transparent, and reproducible synthesis of this literature is still lacking.

Objective: This article provides a comprehensive systematic literature review (SLR) and taxonomy of ER methods in RL. We aim to (i) categorize existing approaches across key methodological dimensions, (ii) quantify publication trends and application domains, and (iii) identify research gaps and reproducibility issues.

Methods: We followed PRISMA guidelines to conduct a structured search across major scientific databases (Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, and Springer). An initial pool of 3600 records was identified, from which 250 studies were assessed in full text after screening. Following quality assessment and snowballing, a final corpus of 200 studies was included in the systematic synthesis.

Results: Our systematic analysis identifies clear methodological trends and clusters in experience replay research. Prioritized replay is the most widely adopted advanced sampling strategy, while hindsight replay predominates in goal-conditioned and sparse-reward tasks. The field also shows growing diversification toward episodic, memory-efficient, and multi-agent replay designs. However, reproducibility remains a concern, with only a minority of studies providing accessible code or complete experimental documentation.

Conclusions: This review provides the first systematic, taxonomy-driven synthesis of ER methods in RL, highlighting both dominant trends and under-explored areas such as uncertainty-aware sampling and scalable memory architectures. All extracted datasets, tables, and screening information are released as open supplementary material to support future research.

Research framing

Research problem

The literature on replay buffers and experience-sampling strategies in reinforcement learning is heterogeneous, making principled comparison and selection difficult.

Technical substance

Main contributions

  • PRISMA-guided synthesis of 200 included studies from an initial search set of 3,600 records.
  • A multi-dimensional taxonomy covering sampling, buffer architecture, goal handling, retention/replacement policy, and design objectives.
  • Quantitative analysis of publication patterns, application domains, empirical evaluation practices, and reproducibility gaps.

Methodological overview

Search and screen→PRISMA eligibility→200-study corpus→Replay-method taxonomy→Trends and open gaps

What the article establishes

Evidence and reported findings

  • The review screens 250 studies in full text and includes 200 studies in the final synthesis.
  • The survey identifies prioritized replay as a major advanced sampling family and hindsight replay as prominent for sparse-reward, goal-conditioned tasks.
  • Its assessment reports limited reproducibility documentation in the surveyed corpus; the paper provides screening and extracted supplementary materials.
Responsible interpretation

Scope and limitations

This is a systematic literature review, not a new replay algorithm or a head-to-head experimental benchmark performed by the authors. Comparisons inherit the heterogeneity and reporting limitations of the studies surveyed.

Research discovery

Research topics where this publication is relevant

  • A taxonomy or methodological comparison of experience replay, sampling policies, or replay memory architectures is needed.
  • A literature review on sample efficiency, off-policy learning, prioritized replay, hindsight replay, or reproducibility in RL is being prepared.
